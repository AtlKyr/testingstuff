[intense] The yield curve: upside down for over two years. Sell on the alarm, A? Or ignore it, B? One all-clear signal decides it. Short rates above long rates: sell. Flip back: buy. Wall Street calls it the recession alarm. Twenty years, ten thousand dollars. Two thousand six: the alarm rings. Sell, buy, sell, buy. Ten sells in a year and a half. And the recession comes. Twenty twenty-two: it sells and sits out over two years. The S and P climbs over forty percent without it. [curious] Now, about that all-clear. June two thousand seven: the curve flips back. It buys. Four months later, the top. It rides the whole crash, down fifty-five percent. Same as doing nothing. [dramatic pause] Twenty years later: alarm, forty-one thousand six hundred. Holder, seventy thousand two hundred. [firm] Right about the recession. Wrong about the money.
