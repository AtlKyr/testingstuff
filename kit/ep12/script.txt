[intense] The S&P just fell over five and a half percent from its record. Was waiting for the dip smart? A: the patient waiter. B: buying at record highs. Guess now. The answer hides in one long wait. Rule: buy after a five percent drop, sell at every new record. Two thousand seven: the waiter sells the top, buys back cheaper, and leads. Two thousand eight crushes them both. Twenty thirteen: records everywhere. Buy and hold catches up. Twenty fifteen: the waiter leads again. Seventeen trades, every one a winner. [dry] Then the dip stopped coming. Remember that long wait? Nineteen months in cash. When the dip came, prices were twenty-five percent higher. Final score. The waiter: forty-nine thousand seven hundred. Buying at records: seventy thousand two hundred. [confident] Perfect trades, still lost. The dip you wait for might never come.
