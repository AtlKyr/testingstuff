[intense] Down two percent, closed green: thirty-one times. Sell the plunge, A? Or hold, B? Wednesday, the Dow erased an eleven-hundred-point drop. But this case turns on the next morning. Our panic seller sells when SPY drops two percent in a day, then buys back next morning. Ten thousand dollars each, twenty years of SPY. At first, panic looks brilliant. Most days closed below where he sold. By December two thousand eight, he's ahead. [dry] Then the bleeding starts. Every escape costs fees. Half the time, he buys back higher. [curious] Now, about that next morning. September eighteenth, two thousand eight: he sells at minus two. The market closes green. Next morning: over ten percent higher. [firm] Twenty years later. Panic seller: thirty-seven thousand five hundred. Holder: seventy thousand two hundred. Panic felt safe. It kept sending bills.
