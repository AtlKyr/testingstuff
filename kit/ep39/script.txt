[curious] Eighty dividend checks in twenty years. Pocket them, A? Or reinvest, B? Watch one tiny check from two thousand eight. Two investors, ten thousand dollars of SPY each, two thousand five. Every three months, SPY pays them. He keeps every check as cash. She buys more SPY with hers. Two thousand seven: neck and neck. Then the crash. And look: his cash pile puts him in the lead. So pocketing wins? [dramatic] Now, that tiny check. December two thousand eight: fifty-nine dollars. She bought SPY with it near the bottom. By twenty twenty-four, it was worth five hundred thirty. Every check kept doing that, quietly, for twenty years. His checks added up to sixty-eight hundred dollars. [dramatic pause] Final tally: pocketed, fifty-five thousand. Reinvested, seventy thousand. The checks were small. What they grew into wasn't.
