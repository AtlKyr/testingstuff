[curious] Twice the S and P. Twice the money? Rich, A? Or wrecked, B? Keep your eye on one March. Two investors, ten thousand dollars each, two thousand five. One buys the S and P. The other buys a fund that moves twice as much, every day. Two thousand six, seven: the double fund pulls ahead. Then two thousand eight. Every one percent drop is two for him. [dramatic] Remember that March? Two thousand nine. His ten thousand had shrunk to two thousand five hundred. He didn't pass the plain fund again until twenty fourteen. Then the bull market, doubled. Twenty twenty-two: brutal again. And costs took their cut, every day. [dramatic pause] Final score: the S and P, seventy-one thousand. Double the S and P, one hundred forty-eight thousand. Twice the money. But first, three quarters of it vanished.
