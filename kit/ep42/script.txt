[curious] A death cross. Sell for a year? Sell, A? Or ignore it, B? Watch one April Fools' Day. The rule: short-term average dips under the long-term one? He sells and sits out a year. Two thousand six: the first cross. He sells. The market climbs without him. December two thousand seven: another cross. He sells right before the crash, and buys back about forty percent cheaper. Now he's way ahead! So the cross works? Twenty ten, twenty eleven: two more crosses. Both times, the market rips higher. His lead is gone. [dramatic] Then comes April Fools' Day, twenty twenty. The cross fires a week after the bottom. He sells. A year later, he buys back over sixty percent higher. [dramatic pause] Final tally: the cross seller, twenty-nine thousand. Ignoring it, seventy-one thousand. Six of his eight sells, he bought back higher.
