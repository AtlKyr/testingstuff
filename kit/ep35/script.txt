One percent fee. Two hundred forty bills. Costs a little, A? Or a lot, B? The worst cost never shows on a bill. Two investors put ten thousand dollars into the S and P in two thousand five. Same fund, but one pays a one percent fee every year. Year one, his bill is about a hundred dollars. Who would notice? Two thousand eight: the market crashes, and the bill still arrives. Then the bull market comes, and the bills grow with it. Now, remember the cost no bill shows? Each fee dollar paid in two thousand five stopped growing. Left invested, it would have become about seven. Final score: no fee, seventy thousand. One percent, fifty-seven thousand five hundred. Bills: four thousand four hundred. Real cost: twelve thousand seven hundred. One percent ate over a fifth of the gains.
