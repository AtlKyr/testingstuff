The S and P: eight percent off its record. Buy monthly, A? Or wait for dips, B? One price tag gives it away. Both invest five hundred a month, but B only buys eight percent below the top. Two thousand five: no dip. B's cash piles up to sixteen thousand dollars. Summer two thousand seven, the dip finally comes, and he spends it all. Then two thousand eight crushes both. Here's the strange part. After the crash, the market calms down, and B waits again. Remember that price tag? He waited two and a half years for a sale, then paid thirty-six percent more than on day one. Every long wait ended that way. Final count: buy monthly, four hundred ninety thousand. Wait for dips, four hundred seventy-two thousand. The sale he waited for cost him almost eighteen thousand.
